Attention training at work: what the research shows

Innerleaps · version 29 October 2026 · replaces "The Business Case for Awareness Interventions"

This document sets out the figures we use for attention training. Every figure appears word for word in the source next to it. Each one also says what kind of study it comes from, and who was measured. Our own estimates and projections are not included.

2. Sickness absence in the Netherlands

  • Absence was 5.6% of working days in the fourth quarter of 2025 (Statistics Netherlands, CBS).
  • One in four days of sickness absence is linked to stress, according to figures from ArboNed and HumanCapitalCare covering about 1 million employees (July 2024 to June 2025).

3. Our own measurement: TheyDo

TheyDo is a fully remote tech scale-up. 39 employees took part in the 6-week training in spring 2026.

  • +8.6% productivity, 4 weeks after the training. Self-reported with the WHO Health and Work Performance Questionnaire, n=14.
  • 68% noticed less stress and tension, 55% less time on autopilot, 55% less mental fatigue, 41% better concentration. Self-reported in the end-of-training evaluation, n=22 of 39, in 5 groups.
  • Trainer rated 9.3 on average, programme 8.1.
Limitations. There is no control group: we compare participants with themselves, before and after. So we can't say whether a change was caused by the training. Everything is self-reported, not everyone filled in the questionnaires, and the groups are small. Results differ per organisation and are no guarantee.

4. When does the training pay for itself?

We don't calculate an expected saving, because we can't promise one. We only calculate the break-even point: the cost of the training against the participants' salaries.

Break-even in productivity = price per participant ÷ average gross annual salary
Break-even in sick days = price per participant ÷ (gross annual salary ÷ 260 working days)

Example: at €575 per participant and an average salary of €40,000, the training pays for itself from 1.44% more productivity, or if each participant has 3.7 fewer sick days a year. At TheyDo the break-even point was 0.36%; participants reported 8.6%. Work it out for your own organisation at innerleaps.nl.
